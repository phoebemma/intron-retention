Running all three gives you a **complete decomposition** of how aging and exercise affect splicing, because each component answers a distinct biological question:
  
  ---
  
  **`zprob` - Probability of perfect splicing**
  - "Does aging/exercise change whether an intron is perfectly spliced at all?"
- This is the binary decision - in/out of the perfectly spliced pool
- Directly comparable to your binomial model results
- Most interpretable for biological audience

---
  
  **`conditional` - Degree of retention given imperfect splicing**
  - "Among introns that are already partially retained, does aging/exercise make retention worse or better?"
- Isolates the retention process from the splicing decision
- Would detect subtle gradations that the binomial model completely misses
- Most sensitive to small changes in the partially retained pool

---
  
  **`response` - Overall marginal prediction**
  - Combines both components into a single retention score
- Answers "what is the net effect of aging/exercise on retention overall?"
- Most useful for ranking introns by total effect size
- Best for volcano plots and reporting top hits

---
  
  **The scientific value of having all three:**
  
  An intron could show:
  - **Effect in `zprob` only** ??? aging pushes introns out of perfect splicing but doesn't change how much they're retained when imperfect
- **Effect in `conditional` only** ??? intron stays imperfectly spliced regardless of age but degree of retention worsens
- **Effect in both** ??? aging both reduces perfect splicing probability AND worsens retention among imperfect - strongest biological signal
- **Opposing effects** ??? which would be missed entirely by `response` alone

That third pattern - opposing effects - is the key reason not to rely on `response` alone.
